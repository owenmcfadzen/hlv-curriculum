PHASE 1 · DAYS 1-30 · VALIDATE
Confirm the problem, then test your solution.
GOAL
Confirm the problem is real, then see if your solution lands.
Start with the problem. A solution to a problem nobody has is wasted work.
HOW
Put it in front of real people.
Use the lo-fi tests from your market week. Watch what they do, not what they say.
TOMÁS, FILLED IN
Do parents agree their teen quits in the first month? Show ten of them the practice-streak idea and see who asks for it.

PHASE 2 · DAYS 30-70 · BETA
Launch the beta and win your first paying users.
GOAL
Get the MVP live and the first 25 users paying.
It need not be full price, but it has to be real money. Paying is the proof it matters.
HOW
Sign people up one by one.
Onboard them yourself, fix what breaks, and ask the ones who pay why they did.
TOMÁS, FILLED IN
Charge parents two euros a month for the beta. Twenty-five of them paying means the problem is worth solving.

PHASE 3 · DAYS 70-100 · SCALE
Find one channel you can afford, then launch.
GOAL
Grow one channel and get the app fully launched.
Move from people who tried it as a favour to customers who pay because they need it.
HOW
Test channels for cost, then commit.
Keep the cost of winning a customer under what your business specialist can afford.
TOMÁS, FILLED IN
Try music-school partnerships and a couple of short video ads. Whichever brings parents in cheapest becomes the channel.

BUDGET
What a budget is, and how to set one.
WHAT IT IS
The money you spend across the hundred days.
Plus what you spend it on. Most of it is small: a few tools, a landing page, a little ad test.
HOW TO SET IT
List each cost, then add it up.
Keep it to money you can actually get. A tight budget forces sharper choices.
TOMÁS'S 100-DAY BUDGET
Nothing to talk to parents, forty euros for a landing page, sixty to test two ad videos. A hundred euros for the whole stretch.
